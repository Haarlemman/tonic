The Tonic and the Bones

Identity is rarely a single line. In the memoir Tonic for the Bones, the narrative is shaped by conflicting pillars that define the search for meaning.

Paradox

Parkinson’s

Diagnosed in 2021, this is the uninvited guest in my creative process. It is a physical paradox: a loss of motor control that has sharpened my mental focus. I’ve stopped trying to silence the symptoms and started using the friction it creates to find a more honest, urgent rhythm in my work.

Outsider

Adoption

I am an adoptee. Being raised outside my biological narrative turned me into a permanent observer, a writer who looks at the world from the outside in. It is the foundation of my belief that identity isn’t just inherited; it is constructed, piece by piece, from the shards.

Question

Jewish Legacy

An ongoing conversation with history, tradition, and a complex present. My stance is hard to categorise—critical of the simple slogans, yet deeply rooted in the ancient necessity of wrestling with the truth.

Fatherhood

The Tonic

My son Yaron, now seven, is the counter-force to adversity. He is the bright spark of humor and intelligence that refocuses the entire narrative. In the chaos of the bones, his presence is the tonic—the reminder that while identity is constructed, love is the mortar that holds the structure firm.
