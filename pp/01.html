A Search for Roots and Reconciliation

Most people have two parents. David Enker has four—or eight, if counting partners[cite: 1]. Relinquished as an infant in Amsterdam, raised with unconditional love by adoptive parents, yet plagued by an underlying sense of uprootedness and a hunger for belonging[cite: 1].

Spanning decades and crossing international borders, Phantom Parents captures the universal quest to reconcile a fragmented past, understand absent figures who loom large like kings or alien beings, and ultimately build a grounded family of one's own.

Across Cities & Memories

The memoir navigates a deeply personal atlas, weaving chronological milestones with geographical waypoints that shaped a creative life.

Amsterdam

Spring 1970 origins on the Onbekende Gracht[cite: 1], early childhood memories, and artistic awakenings.

The Hague

Moving south, family dynamics, the passing of an adoptive father, and meeting biological roots.

Detroit & North America

A life-changing 1993 Greyhound bus journey traversing landscapes, cities, and youthful introspection[cite: 1].

London & Tel Aviv

Surviving the 7/7 London bombings[cite: 1], discovering ancestral Holocaust history, and exploring the Holy Land.
