1. What’s Wrong With Sitting Still?

Most creative tools assume you're seated, still, and typing. But our bodies are made to move—especially when we think, create, or feel. For people with Parkinson’s, this isn't just about comfort. It’s about access, expression, and freedom.

“What if the studio moved with us, instead of pinning us down?”

2. A Studio That Moves

The Standing Studio reimagines how we create — not seated and static, but upright, mobile, and responsive. It's built for real human rhythms, especially those of people with Parkinson’s or other neuro-challenges, where tremors, stiffness, or fatigue can make traditional tools feel like barriers.

Instead of forcing the body to adapt to the machine, the studio adapts to you. It listens to your voice, responds to gestures, and supports movement. You can write text hands-free, navigate creative software by speaking, brainstorm while walking, or build ideas without needing to sit still and type.

This is more than accessibility — it’s about dignity and creative continuity. When we design with the body in mind, everyone — not just those with physical challenges — can stay present, productive, and empowered.

3. Setup Examples

  • Micro Studio: Just a headset, a mic, and smart software.
  • Home Creator Studio: Standing desk + webcam + smart assistant + projector or second screen.
  • Immersive Studio: Full-body motion capture, large projection wall, real-time feedback.
