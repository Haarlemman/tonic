Chapter 1

Introduction

The Joy of Adversity

This is not just a book about Parkinson’s. It’s a book about life—its trials, its joys, and the unexpected lessons that emerge when you’re forced to slow down and truly pay attention.

In 2021, I received my diagnosis: Parkinson’s disease. It hit me like a storm coming through the backdoor, though looking back, the signs had been there for years. At first, it felt like a cruel twist of fate. I was 51, a new father to my son Yaron, and suddenly faced a condition that promised a lifetime of limitations.

But what I didn’t know then—and what I hope to share with you now—is that Parkinson’s also came with unexpected gifts. It became my teacher, revealing truths about resilience, joy, and the power of living in the present.

"Parkinson’s doesn’t define me. It’s just a part of my story—a story I now share with you."

Part I - The Body

The Wrong Diagnosis

Early 2021. The middle of the COVID pandemic. I notice I can’t smell anything. For over a decade, I’ve battled anxiety. Then, my wife notices something strange. A tiny detail. My right arm doesn’t swing when I walk.

Dr. Google suggests something unexpected: *Parkinson’s.* There it is, a typo in disguise, sitting smugly at the top of the search results.

"Life expectancy: only a few years. My wife wants to scream. I just sit there."
Part II - The Mind

The Trauma of Control

We often treat trauma as an event that happened *to* us. But what if the deepest, most persistent trauma is the accumulated energy of *resistance*?

The need for absolute control—the belief in an unfettered free will—is a comforting lie. When the body fails, the mind panics because its master plan is invalidated.

"You are not fighting Parkinson's. You are fighting the old narrative of who you thought you were."
Part III - The Soul

The Paradox of Belonging

We are social animals wired for community, yet we live in an age that celebrates radical individualization. My own political and religious stance is hard to categorize—it’s critical of the left’s embrace of identity fragmentation, yet rooted in a deeply Jewish tradition of argument.

The paradox of belonging is this: you must first find your structure (your Body, Mind, and Soul triad) before you can truly engage with the community. You cannot connect if you are constantly defining yourself by what you are *against*.

Context

The Science & The Symptoms

Parkinson's Disease (PD) is a progressive neurodegenerative disorder primarily affecting the dopamine-producing neurons in a specific area of the brain called the **substantia nigra**.

Key Symptoms

  • **Bradykinesia:** Slowness of movement.
  • **Tremor:** Resting tremors (often starting unilaterally).
  • **Rigidity:** Stiffness of the limbs or trunk.
  • **Non-Motor:** Fading sense of smell, sleep difficulties, and mood disorders (anxiety/depression).

Like an atom losing electrons, the loss of dopamine creates instability in the system.
